Plant lover gifts for the person with 40 plants and names for all of them

Updated 2026-10-23 · Plant People designs · gift guide

Why plant people are the easiest (and hardest) to shop for

If you're shopping for someone who talks to their fiddle leaf fig and has named every pothos cutting, you're looking for plant lover gifts that actually get their obsession. The good news: plant people love anything that acknowledges their hobby. The tricky part: they probably already own three propagation stations and have strong opinions about terracotta versus ceramic.

The best gifts either make their plant care easier, celebrate their ridiculous level of dedication, or make them laugh about how deep into this they've gotten. Skip the generic "plant mom" stuff that feels mass-produced, and go for things that show you understand the difference between someone who owns a succulent and someone who knows all 40 of their plants by name.

Practical plant care tools they&#x27;ll actually use

Someone with 40 plants doesn't need another decorative watering can. They need tools that make the weekly care routine less of a workout. Think about what actually helps when you're managing a small indoor jungle.

  • Moisture meters: Takes the guesswork out of watering. A simple analog version runs $8-15, digital ones with multiple sensors go up to $25-40.
  • Long-spout watering cans: For reaching hanging plants without a step stool. Look for 40-60 oz capacity with a narrow spout. $15-30.
  • Pruning shears and snips: Sharp, comfortable ones make deadheading and trimming painless. Quality sets run $12-25.
  • Plant labels and markers: Because naming plants is one thing; remembering which one is Gregory versus Gerald is another. $8-15 for a set.

Pots and planters for the pot hoarder

Plant people collect pots like some people collect shoes. They have a "pot problem" and they're not sorry about it. The trick is finding ones they don't already own, which means going for unusual sizes, colors, or styles.

Consider cache pots in unexpected colors (rust, sage, charcoal) rather than standard terracotta. Self-watering pots work great for the person who travels but refuses to rehome their collection. Hanging planters in macramé or modern metal styles add vertical space for the inevitable new plants. Price range varies wildly: basic ceramic pots start around $8-15, designer cache pots and self-watering systems run $20-50, and statement planters can go $40-80.

Pro tip: odd sizes (like 3.5-inch or 7-inch) are harder to find in stores and therefore more appreciated than standard 4-inch and 6-inch pots.

Propagation supplies for the plant multiplier

Anyone with 40 plants definitely propagates. They can't help themselves. Every pruning session creates new babies, and every new baby needs somewhere to root.

Glass propagation stations look pretty on a windowsill and let them watch roots develop (plant people find this genuinely exciting). Test tube-style versions run $12-25, larger multi-bulb stations go $20-40. Rooting hormone powder or gel helps cuttings establish faster, around $6-12. Small nursery pots in bulk (because they're always running out) cost $10-20 for a set of 20-50. Clear plastic humidity domes for rooting trays run $8-15.

Books and guides for the plant nerd

The person who names their plants also wants to understand them better. They'll read about soil composition for fun and actually care about the difference between cultivars.

Look for comprehensive care guides organized by plant family, troubleshooting books with clear photos of pest and disease issues, or books about specific plant types they're obsessed with (aroids, hoyas, begonias). Propagation guides with detailed instructions make great gifts. Plant identification books help them figure out what mystery cutting their friend gave them. Most quality plant books run $15-35.

Subscription boxes and ongoing plant lover gift ideas

For the person who always has room for one more plant (narrator: they don't, but they'll make room), subscription gifts keep giving.

Monthly plant subscriptions deliver new specimens to their door, usually $20-45 per month depending on plant size. Seed subscriptions work for anyone who also gardens outdoors, around $10-25 per month. Fertilizer or soil amendment subscriptions ensure they never run out of the good stuff, typically $15-30 per delivery. These gifts work because plant people genuinely never get tired of new plants or supplies.

What to avoid when shopping for serious plant people

Skip anything that treats plants as a passing trend rather than a serious hobby. Cheap plastic pots that crack in six months, flimsy tools that bend on first use, or fake plants (just don't) miss the mark completely.

Avoid generic "plant lady" items covered in basic leaf prints unless they're actually funny or well-designed. The person with 40 named plants has evolved beyond basic plant merch. Also skip live plants unless you know exactly what they want and don't have—nothing's more awkward than gifting someone a plant they've killed twice already or one that doesn't fit their growing conditions.

Finally, steer clear of anything with care instructions that contradict what experienced plant people know. Gifts that assume they're beginners will just gather dust.

Questions people ask

What's a good budget for plant lover gifts?

Most thoughtful plant lover gifts fall between $15-40. Tools and small pots sit at the lower end, while quality planters, books, and apparel land in the $20-35 range. Subscription boxes or premium planters can go higher if you want to splurge.

Should I buy an actual plant as a gift for a plant person?

Only if you know their specific wishlist and growing conditions. Someone with 40 plants has particular tastes and space constraints, so a gift card to their favorite nursery or plant-related gear is usually safer than a surprise plant.

Are funny plant shirts too cheesy for a gift?

Not if they're oddly specific rather than generic. Plant people appreciate humor that shows you understand how deep into the hobby they are—shirts that reference the exact number of plants or naming them hit different than basic "plant mom" puns.
