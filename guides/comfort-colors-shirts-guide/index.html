Comfort Colors shirts: colors, fit and why every Etsy bestseller uses them

Updated 2026-10-23

Why Comfort Colors shirts show up everywhere

If you've ever scrolled through Etsy or shopped at a craft fair, you've probably seen Comfort Colors shirts without realizing it. They're the soft, slightly oversized tees with that lived-in feel and those distinctive, saturated colors. They've become the default blank for custom printers, embroiderers, and small brands because they photograph beautifully, feel great, and have a quality that justifies a higher price point than your standard promotional tee.

The most popular style is the Comfort Colors 1717, a heavyweight 6.1 oz ringspun cotton tee that's garment-dyed. That garment-dye process is what gives these shirts their signature soft hand and slightly faded, vintage look right out of the package. Unlike pigment-dyed shirts that can feel stiff, these get softer with every wash.

The fit: relaxed but not baggy

Comfort Colors tshirts run true to size with a relaxed, boxy fit. They're cut wider through the body and sleeves than a slim-fit tee, but they're not oversized in an intentional streetwear way. Think of them as the shirt you'd grab on a Saturday morning: comfortable without looking like you're swimming in fabric.

The sleeves hit right at mid-bicep, and the body has enough room that it doesn't cling. If you're between sizes, most people stick with their usual size rather than sizing down. The length is standard, hitting right around the hip. After washing, expect about half an inch of shrinkage in length and width, though the garment-dye process means they've already been pre-shrunk once during manufacturing.

For reference, a size large measures roughly 22 inches across the chest and 30 inches in length. The fabric has a slightly uneven texture because of the ringspun cotton, which adds to that worn-in aesthetic.

The color situation (and why it matters for gifts)

Comfort Colors offers around 50 colors, and this is where they really shine. We're not talking about basic red, blue, and black. You get shades like pepper (a dusty greenish-gray), chalky mint, terracotta, chambray (a faded denim blue), and crimson. These are the colors that make a comfort colors mockup look instantly more expensive and gift-worthy.

The garment-dye process means each shirt has slight color variations, and the fabric takes on a heathered, uneven tone that looks intentional. Darker colors like black and navy will have a slightly faded appearance. Lighter colors like ivory and chambray have a softer, almost pastel quality.

If you're ordering a custom-printed shirt as a gift, earth tones and muted colors tend to be the most versatile. Shades like blue jean, moss, and pepper work for almost anyone and pair well with most print colors. Brighter options like lagoon blue or watermelon are great if you know the recipient's style leans playful.

Comfort Colors sweatshirt and crewneck options

The comfort colors crewneck (style 1566) follows the same garment-dyed formula as the tees. It's a 9.5 oz heavyweight fleece with a soft, broken-in feel. Like the shirts, it runs true to size with a relaxed fit. The cuffs and waistband are ribbed but not tight, and the whole thing has that same slightly uneven color saturation.

Crewnecks are ideal for fall and winter gifts, especially with funny or specific designs printed on the front. They're substantial enough to feel like a quality gift but not so precious that someone won't actually wear it. The Gildan 18000 crewneck is another popular option for custom printing; it's lighter weight and less expensive, but it doesn't have that same premium hand-feel.

Both the comfort colors sweatshirt and the Gildan version hold up well to repeated washing. The Comfort Colors option will continue to soften and fade slightly, while the Gildan tends to maintain its original shape and color longer.

What to look for when buying custom-printed Comfort Colors

Not all custom printers use genuine Comfort Colors blanks, and you can usually tell from the product photos. Look for listings that specifically mention "Comfort Colors 1717" or show a comfort colors mockup with that characteristic texture and color depth. If the shirt looks perfectly smooth and evenly colored in the photos, it's probably not Comfort Colors.

Print quality matters more on these shirts because the fabric is so nice. A cheap, cracked print will stand out against the soft, premium feel of the shirt. Look for sellers who use direct-to-garment (DTG) printing or high-quality screen printing. The print should feel soft to the touch, not thick and plasticky.

Expect to pay between $25 and $40 for a custom-printed Comfort Colors shirt, depending on the complexity of the design and the seller's overhead. Anything significantly cheaper is probably using a different blank or cutting corners on print quality.

Seasonal colors: comfort colors halloween and beyond

Comfort Colors releases seasonal shades that show up in custom printing, especially around holidays. For comfort colors halloween, you'll see lots of burnt orange, black, and pepper used for spooky or autumn-themed designs. These colors photograph especially well in fall lighting and pair nicely with orange, black, or cream-colored prints.

Around the winter holidays, ivory, crimson, and forest green become popular. Spring brings out lagoon, chalky mint, and seafoam. If you're ordering a seasonal gift, choosing a color that matches the time of year makes it feel more intentional, even if the design itself isn't holiday-specific.

Keep in mind that some colors are only available seasonally or may be discontinued. If you find a color you love, it's worth noting the specific name so you can request it if you reorder.

Washing and care (they get better with age)

One of the best things about Comfort Colors is that they improve with wear. Wash them in cold water, tumble dry on low, and they'll get softer and slightly more faded each time. The garment-dye process means the color won't wash out dramatically; it just takes on more of that vintage look.

Avoid bleach and harsh detergents, which can cause uneven fading or damage the fabric. If there's a printed design, turn the shirt inside out before washing to protect the print. The fabric itself is durable and won't pill or lose its shape with normal wear.

After a year of regular washing, a Comfort Colors shirt will look like you've owned it forever in the best way possible. The fabric gets that paper-thin softness that makes it your go-to weekend shirt, and the print will have a slightly worn, lived-in quality that actually adds to the charm.

Questions people ask

Do Comfort Colors shirts shrink?

They're garment-dyed, which pre-shrinks them during manufacturing, but expect about half an inch of additional shrinkage in length and width after the first wash. Washing in cold water and tumble drying on low minimizes this.

Why are Comfort Colors shirts more expensive than regular tees?

They're made from heavyweight ringspun cotton and go through a garment-dye process that gives them a soft, vintage feel right away. The quality and unique colors justify the higher price for custom printers and buyers.

What's the difference between Comfort Colors 1717 and other styles?

The 1717 is their classic short-sleeve tee and the most popular style for custom printing. Other styles include long sleeves, pocket tees, and tank tops, all using the same garment-dyed ringspun cotton.
