Pickleball gifts that get used, not re-gifted

Updated 2026-10-23 · Pickleball Players designs · gift guide

Why most pickleball gifts miss the mark

If you're shopping for someone who plays pickleball four mornings a week and can't stop talking about their last game, you need pickleball gifts that actually get used. The problem is most gift guides push generic sports gear or decorative items that collect dust. What pickleball addicts really want are things that make their court time better, funnier, or more comfortable—gifts they'll reach for every single time they play.

The best gifts either solve a real problem (sweaty grips, lost balls, sun in their eyes) or celebrate the obsession in a way that makes them laugh. Here's what actually works.

Paddle accessories that improve every game

Serious players go through certain accessories constantly, which makes them perfect gifts. Overgrips are the most practical—these thin wraps go over the paddle handle and need replacing every few weeks depending on play frequency. A pack of 10 to 20 costs $15 to $25 and will be genuinely appreciated.

Paddle edge guards protect the paddle rim from court scrapes and extend its life. They're inexpensive ($8 to $15) and show you understand the game. Paddle covers with extra pockets for balls, keys, and phones ($20 to $35) are another winner, especially padded ones that protect their expensive paddle during transport.

Grip tape in fun colors or patterns ($12 to $18) lets players customize their paddle while improving hold. It's a small gift that feels personal.

Court gear they&#x27;ll actually wear

Pickleball players need sun protection and sweat management, which makes functional clothing a smart gift category. Wide-brimmed hats with moisture-wicking bands ($18 to $30) are essential for outdoor players. A pickleball hat with a funny saying or court-specific design gets worn constantly and sparks conversations between games.

Moisture-wicking socks designed for court sports ($12 to $20 per pair) prevent blisters during long play sessions. Look for ones with arch support and cushioned soles. Sweatbands and wristbands ($8 to $15) might seem old-school, but players who sweat through their grips swear by them.

Visors are popular with players who want sun protection without the heat of a full hat ($15 to $25). The key is finding ones that stay put during quick movements at the kitchen line.

The best pickleball shirts have inside jokes that only players understand—references to dinking, the kitchen, or that 'just one more game' mentality that turns a quick morning session into three hours. A pickleball shirt with a clever saying gets worn to the courts, to the grocery store, and becomes part of their identity.

Sweatshirts ($28 to $40) work for cool morning games, while tees ($18 to $28) are perfect for summer play. Mugs ($12 to $18) with pickleball humor get used every single morning before they head to the courts. The key is finding designs that are funny without being cheesy—specific enough to feel personal to the sport.

Balls and ball holders (yes, really)

Pickleballs seem cheap until you realize how many get lost, cracked, or worn down. A case of 50 outdoor balls ($45 to $70) or 12 indoor balls ($20 to $30) is an incredibly practical gift. Outdoor balls are harder and have smaller holes; indoor balls are softer with larger holes. Know which type your recipient plays with most.

Ball holders that attach to bags or belts ($10 to $18) are surprisingly useful. They hold 2 to 4 balls and mean players always have extras without digging through their bag. Some have clips that attach to court fences.

A ball hopper or basket ($25 to $45) is perfect for players who practice serves or play with groups. It holds 50+ balls and has a hinged lid that makes pickup easy. This is especially appreciated by anyone who coaches or organizes games.

Bags that carry everything

Most players start with a paddle and a grocery bag, then quickly realize they need something better. A dedicated pickleball bag ($35 to $80) has compartments for paddles, balls, water bottles, towels, and personal items. Look for ones with ventilated pockets for sweaty clothes and exterior loops for hanging towels.

Backpack-style bags ($40 to $70) are ideal for players who bike or walk to courts. Tote-style bags ($30 to $55) work better for car transport and quick access. The best bags have padded paddle sleeves that protect against damage.

Insulated pockets for water bottles are a must for outdoor players. Some bags even have separate shoe compartments, which matters if they're heading to the courts directly from work or errands.

Training aids for the competitive player

For players who want to improve their game, training equipment makes a thoughtful gift. Portable nets ($120 to $250) let them practice at home or set up games anywhere. They fold up for transport and take about 5 minutes to assemble.

Target practice nets ($30 to $60) help with accuracy training. These are smaller nets with target zones that improve shot placement. They're especially useful for working on dinks and drop shots.

Court markers or boundary lines ($20 to $40) let players set up regulation courts on any flat surface. Rebound boards ($80 to $150) are perfect for solo practice—they return the ball so players can work on volleys and reaction time without a partner.

Small gifts and stocking stuffers

Sometimes you need something under $20 that still shows you understand their obsession. A pickleball ornament ($8 to $15) is perfect for players who want their tree to reflect their favorite sport. Look for ones shaped like paddles, balls, or court scenes.

Pickleball-themed keychains with paddle or ball designs ($6 to $12) are inexpensive but get used daily. Stickers for water bottles, cars, or paddle cases ($3 to $8 per pack) let them show off their obsession everywhere.

Towels with funny pickleball sayings ($12 to $20) are practical for wiping down during games. Hand warmers ($8 to $15) are appreciated by players in colder climates who play year-round. A simple ball of practice balls in fun colors ($15 to $25) works as a small but useful gift.

Questions people ask

What do you get someone who is obsessed with pickleball?

Get them something they'll use at the courts—accessories like overgrips, paddle covers, or moisture-wicking gear, or something that celebrates their obsession like a funny shirt with pickleball inside jokes. Avoid purely decorative items unless they specifically collect them.

How much should I spend on a pickleball gift?

Most practical pickleball gifts range from $15 to $50, with quality paddles and bags going up to $100+. Small accessories like grips and balls ($10 to $25) make great additions to larger gifts or work as standalone presents for casual gift-giving occasions.

What's the difference between indoor and outdoor pickleball gifts?

The main difference is in the balls—outdoor balls are harder with smaller holes, while indoor balls are softer. For other gifts like paddles, bags, and clothing, most work for both settings, though outdoor players especially appreciate sun protection gear like hats and visors.
